A rank-300 copy is a good way to save space
The SVD was computed wrongly

Answer C. 600 × 400 / 1,001 ≈ 240: past that k the copy is larger than the image.

Block A · recommenders

A ratings matrix is close to low rank because tastes are few

  • Rows are people, columns are items; most entries are missing
  • A rank-k model: each person is k numbers of taste, each item k numbers of character
  • A predicted rating is the dot product of the two
  • Truncated SVD after filling the gaps is the simplest version; methods that fit only the observed entries do better

Link to week 1: a prediction is a dot product of two vectors, a person's and an item's. The lab uses the course-mean fill and the SVD; the stretch fits only observed entries by alternating least squares (week 5).

Choosing k

Choose the rank on ratings the model did not see

Training error

Falls with every extra layer, down to zero at full rank. It cannot choose k.

Held-out error

Falls, then rises once extra layers fit noise. Its lowest point is the rank to use.

The trap. Choosing k on the test set and then reporting the test error flatters the model. With enough data, keep a third split for the final report.

Do not give the lab's best rank: ask the room to predict the shape of the curve and where its minimum might be.

Model adaptation

A low-rank update adapts a large model by training two thin matrices

W' = W + B A, with B d × r and A r × d, W frozen

d = 1,024, r = 8: 16,384 trainable numbers against 1,048,576: 1.56%

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)

The 2021 paper that introduced the method reports up to 10,000 times fewer trainable parameters on its largest model.

From slide_numbers.py, week 8, and Hu et al., "LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models", arXiv 2106.09685 (2021). Sources: the arXiv abstract page. The bet is that the change a task needs is close to low rank; the paper tests it, this course only shows the arithmetic.

Block B · vectorisation and broadcasting

Broadcasting lines shapes up from the right and stretches dimensions of 1

(3, 1) + (1, 2) gives (3, 2):

a = np.array([[1], [2], [3]])   # (3, 1)
b = np.array([[10, 20]])        # (1, 2)
a + b   # [[11, 21], [12, 22], [13, 23]]
  1. Compare shapes from the last dimension
  2. Equal sizes match; a size of 1 stretches
  3. Anything else is an error

From slide_numbers.py, week 8. Week 2 met vectorisation for speed; here the rules, because a wrong broadcast does not always raise an error.

Silent errors

A square test matrix hides an axis bug

  • M - M.mean(axis=1) on an n × m matrix lines the row means up with the columns
  • If n ≠ m it fails with an error; if n = m it runs and is wrong
  • Use keepdims=True, or a shape that says what you mean: (n, 1)
  • Test with rectangular shapes, such as 2 × 3, never only square ones

This is the shape of the planted bug in the lab: let participants find it themselves. Say only that it is a broadcast that runs.
