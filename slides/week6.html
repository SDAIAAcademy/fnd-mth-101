Block A · eigenvectors

An eigenvector is a direction the matrix only stretches

A = [[4, 1], [1, 4]]

A (1, 0) = (4, 1): turned, not an eigenvector

A (1, 1) = (5, 5) = 5 × (1, 1): an eigenvector, eigenvalue 5

A v = λ v

λ is the stretch factor. A negative λ flips the direction; λ = 0 squashes it: the matrix is singular.

From slide_numbers.py, week 6. Ask for the other eigenvector: (1, −1), with A(1, −1) = (3, −3), eigenvalue 3.

By hand

Eigenvalues solve det(A − λI) = 0

det([[4 − λ, 1], [1, 4 − λ]]) = (4 − λ)² − 1 = 0

4 − λ = ±1, so λ = 5 or 3

For a 2 × 2: λ² − (trace) λ + det = 0. Here trace 8, det 15.

w, V = np.linalg.eigh(A)
w          # [3., 5.]  ascending
V[:, 1]    # [0.7071, 0.7071]

From slide_numbers.py, week 6. eigh is for symmetric matrices: real eigenvalues in ascending order, orthonormal eigenvectors. For larger matrices nobody uses the polynomial: libraries iterate.

Symmetric matrices

A symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors

  • A = Q Λ Qᵀ: rotate into the eigenvector basis, stretch each axis, rotate back
  • (1, 1)/√2 and (1, −1)/√2 are orthogonal, as week 4 would want
  • Covariance matrices and XᵀX are symmetric: everything this week applies to them
Machine learning use. The eigenvalues of XᵀX are the squares of X's singular values; their ratio is cond(X)², week 5's number.

The spectral theorem, stated not proved. Link back: a zero eigenvalue of XᵀX is a dependent column of X (week 3).

Block C · power iteration

Multiply by the matrix again and again, and the leading eigenvector wins

  • Write the start as a mix of eigenvectors
  • Each multiplication scales each part by its eigenvalue
  • The part with the largest |λ| outgrows the rest; normalise to keep the numbers in range
  • The error shrinks by about |λ₂ / λ₁| per step

No eigenvalue formula needed: just products. This is how a matrix with millions of rows has its leading eigenvector found.

What goes wrong

Dead ends, closed loops and link farms break the plain walk; damping repairs it

ProblemEffectRepair
A page with no links outProbability leaks awayJump anywhere from it
A closed group of pagesThe walk is trapped, may cycleDamping: jump anywhere with probability 1 − d
Pages that link to each other to lift oneThe group gains rankSmaller d limits it; it does not remove it

With damping d, every entry of G is positive, the eigenvalue 1 is unique and the others are at most d in size, so convergence takes about log(tolerance) / log(d) steps: about 142 for 10⁻¹⁰ at d = 0.85 (slide_numbers.py). The lab measures the real count.

Checkpoint

Why does damping speed up convergence?

With damping d = 0.85, power iteration converges in about a hundred steps; at d = 0.99 it takes many more. Why?

A larger d removes pages from the graph
A larger d makes the leading eigenvalue larger than 1
A larger d needs a better starting vector
The second-largest eigenvalue is at most d, so the error shrinks more slowly when d is near 1
Why. The error shrinks by about |λ₂| per step, and |λ₂| ≤ d: 0.99 shrinks it by only 1% a step.

Answer D. The lab's stretch checks it.

Week 6 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Directions that only stretch

Eigenvectors; symmetric matrices have orthogonal ones and real eigenvalues.

Spread has a direction

The leading eigenvector of a covariance matrix is the direction of greatest variance.

Repeat to find it

Power iteration finds the leading eigenvector by products alone; damping makes it converge.
