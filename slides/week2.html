Which pair is most similar by cosine?

Three vectors: a = (2, 0), b = (10, 1), c = (1, 1). Which pair has the largest cosine similarity?

a and b, because their dot product, 20, is the largest
a and b, at a cosine of about 0.995
a and c, because they have similar lengths
b and c, because both have a second coordinate of 1
Why. cos(a, b) = 20 / (2 × 10.05) ≈ 0.995; cos(a, c) ≈ 0.707; cos(b, c) = 11 / (10.05 × 1.414) ≈ 0.774.

Answer B. Options A and B name the same pair: only B gives the right reason. Ask a participant who chose A to compute cos(a, b) and see that the conclusion holds for another reason.

Block A · the product

Entry (i, j) of AB is row i of A dotted with column j of B

P = [[2, 1], [1, 3]] (2 × 2), G = [[1, 0, 1], [2, 1, 0]] (2 × 3)

Entry (1, 1): (2, 1) · (1, 2) = 4

P G = [[4, 1, 2], [7, 3, 1]], a 2 × 3 matrix

Shapes first

(n × k)(k × m) gives n × m. The inner sizes must match. G P does not exist here: 3 does not match 2.

Numbers from slide_numbers.py, week 2. Work entry (2, 1) with the room: (1, 3) · (1, 2) = 7. Then ask for the shape before any arithmetic: that habit catches most errors.

Many dot products

Stacking vectors as rows turns all their dot products into one product

  • Questions as rows of Q (q × d), articles as rows of A (a × d)
  • Q Aᵀ is q × a: entry (i, j) is question i dotted with article j
  • Week 1's search for one question was one row of this matrix
Why it matters. One product call replaces q × a separate dot products, and the computer runs it far faster.

Draw Q with q rows and Aᵀ with a columns; point at one entry and say which row and column made it. This is the bridge from week 1 to attention.

Numbers from slide_numbers.py, week 2. The division by √d keeps scores from growing with the dimension, so the softmax does not saturate. This course stops at the scores and weights; how a transformer uses them is for the engineering courses.

Block B · maps

A matrix is a map: it sends each vector to a new vector

  • Its columns are where the axis vectors land
  • Rot = [[0, −1], [1, 0]] sends (1, 0) to (0, 1): a quarter turn
  • Ref = [[1, 0], [0, −1]] flips the second coordinate: a mirror

Read the columns

To know what a 2 × 2 matrix does, draw where (1, 0) and (0, 1) go. Every other vector follows by adding and scaling.

Sketch the unit square and its image under Rot and under Ref. Ask: what does [[2, 0], [0, 2]] do? It doubles every vector.

Composition

A product of matrices is one map applied after another, read right to left

MatrixMeansSends (1, 1) to
Ref Rotturn first, then mirror(−1, −1)
Rot Refmirror first, then turn(1, 1)
Order matters. Ref Rot ≠ Rot Ref. Matrix products are not commutative, because doing two things in another order is another map.

Numbers from slide_numbers.py, week 2. Act it out with a sheet of paper: turn then flip, flip then turn. Then show that the product matrix gives the same answer as applying the two in turn.

In machine learning

Stacked linear layers compose into one matrix

  • Two linear layers W₂(W₁ x) equal one layer (W₂ W₁) x
  • Without a non-linear step between them, depth adds nothing
  • Shapes chain: (m × h)(h × d) maps d inputs to m outputs
Cost still counts. W₂(W₁ x) and (W₂ W₁) x give the same result with different numbers of operations. Block C counts them.

This is why networks put a non-linear function between layers. Keep it to the linear fact; the non-linear part belongs to later courses.

Block C · cost

An (n × k)(k × m) product costs 2·n·k·m floating-point operations

n·m entries, each a dot product of length k: k multiplications and k additions.

(3 × 4)(4 × 2): 2 × 3 × 4 × 2 = 48 FLOPs.

A first look at vectorisation

A Python loop pays for every number; a vectorised call pays once

# loop: one Python step per multiply
for i in range(n):
    for j in range(m):
        C[i][j] = sum(A[i][t] * B[t][j]
                      for t in range(k))

# vectorised: one call
C = A @ B
  • Same arithmetic, same FLOP count
  • The call runs compiled code over whole blocks of memory
  • Week 8 returns to vectorisation and broadcasting in full

Do not give the lab's speed-up now: the lab measures it. Ask the room to guess the factor on their laptop and write the guess down; compare at the debrief.

How much does doubling the size cost?

Multiplying two 500 × 500 matrices takes 0.25 GFLOPs. About how many FLOPs does a 1,000 × 1,000 product take?

0.5 GFLOPs
1 GFLOP
4 GFLOPs
2 GFLOPs
Why. 2n³: doubling n multiplies n³ by 8, and 8 × 0.25 = 2.

Answer D. Check with the formula: 2 × 1,000³ = 2 × 10⁹.

Week 2 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Shapes first

(n × k)(k × m) is n × m. Check shapes before arithmetic.

Right to left

A product of matrices applies the right-hand map first. Order changes the map.

Count, then time

2·n·k·m FLOPs is fixed by the maths; the rate is set by how the code runs.

Ask for one example of each from the lab.
